# Gate G2: parity CUDA backend, release 0.2.0

Oct 26, 2026 · Phase 2 of the [execution roadmap](../execution-roadmap.md)

**Decision: approved.** PyFWI runs on CUDA (`PYFWI_BACKEND=cuda`, CuPy from the `cuda13` or `cuda12`
extra) with the kernel files of OpenCL, within recorded tolerances of OpenCL on the whole matrix, and
faster than OpenCL in every scenario. The `fixed` numeric mode, the default of CUDA, corrects the
numerical bugs of 0.1.10 that the phase found and is 7.5 to 15.9 times faster than 0.1.10. OpenCL stays
the default backend, in the `legacy` mode, with exactly the results of 0.1.10. Five open points, none of
which blocks Phase 3, are listed at the end; the first two depend on the project owner.

## What Phase 2 changed and found

- **One engine, two backends.** The time loops moved into `PyFWI.engine.propagation` behind a small
  backend interface (2.1); a selector picks OpenCL or CUDA (2.2); the CUDA backend compiles the same
  `.cl` files with NVRTC after a portability header (2.3). The public API is unchanged and checked on
  both backends (2.5).
- **Numeric modes (2.4).** `legacy` keeps the arithmetic of 0.1.10 token for token after the C
  preprocessor; `fixed` corrects what the phase measured, each correction with a known-bug test that
  passes in `fixed` and fails in `legacy`: float constants and the full `dt`; `vz` in the wells; no
  access outside the buffers but the row past the field edge; the source resampled to every internal
  step (0.1.10's impulse train excited a grid-scale noise: seismograms 3 to 6 % off at a 10 m grid,
  dominated by noise at 5 m); residual and recording rows aligned when rounding makes `nt_ext` differ
  from the recorded steps (a 54 % gradient change at `t` = 0.4 s); the Taylor order-8 coefficients
  (0.1.10's rounded ones make every order-8 wave 0.14 % fast and the scheme second order); the
  gradient loop as the adjoint of the forward step, the adjoint absorbing layer and the shear strain
  of `Grad`; optimisers independent of the data amplitude.
- **The mathematical baseline was partly the check itself.** The 31 % gradient error of 0.1.10 came
  mostly from the absorbing layer moving with `max(vp)` in the finite differences; with it pinned,
  0.1.10 is 6.9 % (vp), 60 % (vs) and 135 % (density) off, and `fixed` 0.6 %, 0.27 % and 1.4 %. New
  metrics: the one-step adjoint dot product (4.3e-3 → 1.1e-7) and the spatial convergence order (order
  8: 2.2 → 7.5).
- **Performance.** CUDA `fixed` is 7.5× to 15.9× faster than 0.1.10. The parity backend became faster
  than OpenCL once its copies went through pinned buffers and its blocks were sized to the grid.

## Open points

1. **Self-hosted GPU runner** not registered (owner action, unchanged since G0). Every commit of Phase
   2 ran the fast suite on NVIDIA and on POCL, the reference suite and the slow suite on the RTX before
   being pushed. How to register it: [self-hosted-runner.md](../self-hosted-runner.md).
2. **Nsight Compute** still cannot read the GPU counters (owner action, roadmap pending action 1). Phase
   3 needs them to measure the bandwidth of the optimised kernels.
3. **Review of the `fixed` results by a geophysicist.** The `fixed` mode changes results (propagation
   cases: seismograms up to 6 %, raw gradients up to 45 %; inversions: models 1e-4 to 9e-4), every
   change measured as more accurate against analytic solutions and finite differences. OpenCL keeps
   `legacy` as its default until this review.
4. **Row past the field edge.** The stencils of both modes still read one row past the field edge (the
   zero margin acts as the outer boundary); explicit halo cells come with the CUDA kernels of Phase 3.
5. **Lazy `W.W`.** The checkpoints are host arrays filled during the run on both backends; copying them
   only when accessed goes with device-resident checkpoints (step 3.8).
